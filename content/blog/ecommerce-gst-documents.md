If you are planning to sell on Amazon, Flipkart, or Meesho, one of the first compliance steps you cannot skip is GST registration. Under current marketplace rules, GST is mandatory for e-commerce sellers on these platforms—regardless of whether your turnover crosses the standard threshold. In simple terms: no GST, no selling.

The good news? The registration process is straightforward if you have the right documents in hand. This guide walks you through exactly what you need to prepare before starting your GST application, so you can avoid delays, rejections, and back-and-forth with tax authorities.

## Why GST is Mandatory for E-commerce Sellers

For regular offline businesses, GST registration is typically required once annual turnover exceeds ₹40 lakh (₹20 lakh for services). E-commerce sellers do not get that luxury. If you sell goods or services through an e-commerce operator like Amazon, Flipkart, or Meesho, GST registration is compulsory from the very first sale, irrespective of turnover.

This rule exists because the marketplace acts as a tax collector. These platforms are required to deduct Tax Collected at Source (TCS) on every sale and deposit it with the government. For that deduction to work smoothly, the seller must hold a valid GSTIN. Without one, you cannot list products, receive payments, or generate invoices through these marketplaces.

## Documents Required for GST Registration as an E-commerce Seller

Before you begin the application on the GST portal, gather the following documents. Most of these will need to be uploaded as scanned copies in PDF or JPEG format, so keep clean, readable versions ready.

### 1. PAN Card (Mandatory)

Your Permanent Account Number is the foundation of your GST registration. For a sole proprietor, this means your personal PAN. For a partnership firm, LLP, or private limited company, the PAN of the business entity is required.

Ensure the PAN is active and the name on it matches your other documents. A mismatch is one of the most common reasons for GST application rejection.

### 2. Bank Account Details

You need to provide your business bank account details, including:

- Bank account number and IFSC code
- A cancelled cheque (or a bank statement or passbook front page) showing your account details, name, and IFSC

The bank account name should match the name of the business entity or the proprietor's name as reflected in the PAN. If you are selling as a sole proprietor, your personal bank account works. For companies or LLPs, a current account in the business's name is typically required.

### 3. Address Proof

You need to prove where your business is located. Depending on your situation, one of the following will serve as address proof:

- **Owned premises:** A copy of the property tax receipt, electricity bill (not older than 2 months), or municipal khata certificate
- **Rented premises:** A rental agreement or lease deed along with the latest utility bill (electricity, water, or landline) in the business's name. If the utility bill is in the landlord's name, you will also need a No Objection Certificate (NOC) from the owner.
- **Shared or co-working space:** A letter of allotment or an agreement from the co-working operator, along with a utility bill and NOC from the operator

The address you provide will be printed on your GST certificate, so ensure it is accurate and matches your supporting documents.

### 4. NOC from the Property Owner (No Objection Certificate)

This is a critical document that many first-time applicants overlook. If your business premises are rented, leased, or shared—even if you are using a family member's property—you need a signed NOC from the property owner.

The NOC is a simple letter stating that the owner has no objection to the property being used as the registered place of business for your GST registration. It should include:

- The owner's name and signature
- The full address of the property
- Your business name
- A clear statement granting permission for the property's use as a business premises

Without this NOC, your application will likely be flagged or rejected, especially if the utility bill you submit is not in your name.

### 5. Additional Documents (Depending on Business Type)

While the above four are the core requirements, you may need a few extras based on your business structure:

| Business Type | Additional Documents |
|---|---|
| Sole Proprietor | Aadhaar card of the proprietor; passport-size photograph |
| Partnership Firm | Partnership deed, Aadhaar and PAN of all partners, proof of appointment of authorized signatory |
| Private Limited / LLP | Certificate of Incorporation, MOA/AOA or LLP agreement, PAN card of the entity, Aadhaar and PAN of all directors/designated partners, board resolution appointing the authorized signatory |

### 6. Digital Signature (DSC)

If you are registering as a company or an LLP, a Class 2 Digital Signature Certificate is required to sign the GST application electronically. Sole proprietors and partnerships can use Aadhaar-based OTP verification instead, which is faster and requires no additional cost.

## Common Mistakes That Delay GST Registration

Even with all documents ready, small errors can cause delays. Watch out for these:

- **Name mismatches:** The legal name on your PAN, bank account, and address proof must match. If you use a trade name on marketplaces, declare it as the "trade name" in the application while keeping the legal name consistent.
- **Blurry scans:** Upload clear, legible copies. Rejected scans are the most avoidable delay.
- **Incomplete NOC:** A vague or unsigned NOC will not pass scrutiny. Include all required details.
- **Wrong business address:** If you are selling from home, that address is valid as long as you provide the relevant proof and an NOC if needed. Do not use a friend's office address just to appear more established—it creates compliance problems later.

## How the Process Works

Once you have all your documents, here is what the process looks like:

1. **Prepare your documents** as per the list above.
2. **Visit the GST portal** and fill Form REG-01. You will need to select your business type and provide details about your constitution, promoter, PAN, and business address.
3. **Upload the required documents** for each section of the application.
4. **Verify via OTP or DSC** depending on your business type.
5. **Receive an Application Reference Number (ARN)** once submitted. The GST officer will review your application. You may be asked for clarification or additional documents.
6. **Receive your GSTIN** upon approval. You can then download your GST registration certificate.
